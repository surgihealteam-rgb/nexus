What is a loan against property (lap)?

A loan against property lets you borrow against a house, shop, office or plot you already own, while continuing to use it. Rates are lower than unsecured loans, tenures are longer, and loan amounts are higher because the property is the security.

LAP underwriting looks at property value and title as carefully as it looks at your income. NEXUS helps you shortlist lenders by property type and use of funds, prepare the document set and understand fees. The lender takes the credit and legal decisions.

Who can apply

  • Business owners needing growth or working capital
  • Families funding education, weddings or medical expenses
  • Borrowers consolidating high-interest debt
  • Property owners wanting large funds at lower rates than a personal loan

Features and benefits

  • Higher amounts and lower rates than unsecured loans
  • Flexible end-use for lawful purposes
  • Long tenure keeps EMIs low
  • Salaried and self-employed borrowers both eligible
  • Can often be combined with overdraft-style facilities

Eligibility criteria

Each lender sets its own rules. These are typical conditions:

  • Property must be self-owned with clear, marketable title
  • Residential and commercial properties are preferred; some lenders accept industrial
  • Age typically 25 to 65 at maturity
  • Stable income documented through ITR or salary
  • Credit score 700+ is commonly needed

Documents required

Keep these ready to avoid delays. Your lender may ask for more depending on your profile. Use our document checklist tool for a printable list.

  • PAN, Aadhaar and address proof
  • Title deeds, sale deed, previous chain documents
  • Property tax receipts, building plan approval
  • Encumbrance certificate (typically 13 to 30 years)
  • ITR for 2 to 3 years, bank statements for 6 to 12 months
  • Business proof, GST and financials for self-employed applicants

Fees and charges to look out for

  • Processing fee: commonly 0.5% to 2% of the loan plus GST
  • Legal, valuation and technical charges at actuals
  • Prepayment charges on fixed-rate loans or business loans, where applicable
  • Stamp duty and mortgage registration, as per state rules

Smart tips before you apply

  • Be clear on the end use; some lenders restrict speculative uses
  • Do not mortgage the only home you live in unless you are comfortable with the risk
  • Compare the loan-to-value, not just the rate: a higher LTV can mean more usable cash
  • Get a lawyer to review the mortgage agreement

Frequently asked questions

How much can I borrow against my property?
Typically 50% to 70% of the property's market value, depending on property type, location, income and the lender's valuation.
Can I take LAP on a property with an existing home loan?
Usually not on the same property unless the home loan is closed or transferred. A top-up home loan may be a better route.
Is LAP available without ITR?
A few lenders offer low-documentation LAP using bank statements and other proofs, but rates are typically higher. We explain the options after reviewing your profile.
What are the risks?
If you default, the lender can enforce its security interest under the SARFAESI Act. Borrow only what you can service from stable income.
How long does LAP take to disburse?
Legal and technical checks usually take longer than for unsecured loans, often 1 to 3 weeks or more depending on documents and the lender.

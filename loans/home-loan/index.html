What is a home loan?

A home loan is a long-term secured loan used to buy, build, extend or renovate a residential property. The property itself is the security, so interest rates are among the lowest of all retail loans and tenures can stretch to 30 years.

Because the amount is large and the paperwork heavy, the right guidance matters: choosing between banks and housing finance companies, fixed or floating rates, joint or single applicant, and understanding legal and technical checks on the property. NEXUS helps you prepare, compare and connect with partner lenders. The lender makes the final decision.

Typical amount₹5 lakh to ₹5 crore+ (up to about 75% to 90% of property value, as per RBI loan-to-value norms)
TenureUp to 30 years
Indicative rateRoughly 7.5% to 10.5% p.a., depending on the lender, your credit score, loan size and whether the rate is linked to an external benchmark. Check the APR on the KFS.

Who can apply

  • First-time home buyers and upgraders
  • Salaried and self-employed borrowers planning to buy a ready, under-construction or resale home
  • Owners wanting to build on a plot or extend an existing house
  • Existing borrowers paying a high rate who want to transfer

Features and benefits

  • Long tenure up to 30 years keeps EMIs manageable
  • Floating rates linked to an external benchmark (repo rate) for transparency
  • Tax benefits under applicable sections of the Income Tax Act, subject to prevailing law
  • Part-prepayment on floating-rate loans to individuals carries no prepayment charge under RBI rules
  • Joint applications can raise eligibility and tax benefits

Eligibility criteria

Each lender sets its own rules. These are typical conditions:

  • Age typically 21 to 65 at maturity
  • Stable income: salaried with 1+ year experience or self-employed with 2+ years of business
  • Credit score 700+ is commonly needed; 750+ gets better pricing
  • Property must be legally clear with approved plans and a marketable title
  • EMI plus existing obligations usually within 40% to 55% of net income

Documents required

Keep these ready to avoid delays. Your lender may ask for more depending on your profile. Use our document checklist tool for a printable list.

  • PAN, Aadhaar, passport-size photographs
  • Salary slips, Form 16 and 6 months bank statements (salaried)
  • ITR with computation, balance sheet, P&L and bank statements (self-employed)
  • Sale agreement or allotment letter, builder-buyer agreement
  • Title documents, approved building plan, encumbrance certificate
  • Property tax receipts, NOC from society or builder, as required

Fees and charges to look out for

  • Processing fee: typically 0.25% to 1% of the loan plus GST
  • Legal and technical valuation charges, charged at actuals
  • Stamp duty and registration, payable to the state government
  • No prepayment penalty on floating-rate loans taken by individuals
  • Conversion fee, if you switch from one rate type to another

Smart tips before you apply

  • Keep your down payment (typically 10% to 25%) and registration costs ready before applying
  • Compare the effective cost including processing fees, not only the headline rate
  • Check the lender's list of approved projects for under-construction properties
  • Get a sanction letter before paying the builder, and pay only against construction milestones

Frequently asked questions

How much home loan can I get?
Lenders typically fund up to 75% to 90% of the property value depending on the loan amount and income. The actual amount depends on your income, existing EMIs, age, credit score and the lender's valuation of the property.
Which is better: fixed or floating rate?
Floating-rate loans follow market rates and are common for long tenures. Fixed rates give certainty for an initial period and then often reset. Compare the rate-reset terms in the sanction letter.
Can I take a home loan jointly?
Yes. Co-applicants, usually spouse or parents, add their income to eligibility, and co-owners may share tax benefits subject to the law.
What is a pre-approved home loan?
It is an in-principle approval based on your income and credit profile before you choose a property. It helps you negotiate with sellers, but final sanction depends on the property.
What happens if I miss EMIs?
Late payment charges apply and your credit score can drop. Repeated defaults can lead to recovery action under the SARFAESI Act. Talk to your lender early about restructuring if needed.
Does NEXUS help with legal checking of the property?
We guide you on the documents the lender will check. The lender's own legal and technical team verifies the property, and we recommend consulting a property lawyer before paying a token amount.
